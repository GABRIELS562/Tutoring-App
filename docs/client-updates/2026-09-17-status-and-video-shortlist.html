Where the Angelo app stands, and what we need from you

An honest read of the codebase as of 17 September 2026: what is built and working, what is scaffolded but not finished, and the decisions that are now blocking progress.

Part 1 is the build status and the questions we need answered. Running costs sets out what will incur a bill. Part 2 is the video shortlist the questions refer to. Prepared from a direct review of the source code, not from a feature list. Percentages are an engineering estimate of effort remaining, not a promise of dates.

Headline

The software shell is roughly 70% done. The curriculum is roughly 15% done — but because the plan is to curate existing YouTube lessons played inside the app (already built) rather than write lessons from scratch, the remaining curriculum work is curation hours, not authoring. Overall the product is about 45–50% of the way to something you could charge for.

One thing YouTube will not let us do. Embedded YouTube players must keep the YouTube logo, a "Watch on YouTube" link and any ads the channel runs; YouTube's terms forbid hiding these. Students will stay in the app by default, but a determined tap can always open YouTube. We will remove the app's own "Open in YouTube" link, which currently invites exactly that.

The important caveat. On the development machine the app has only ever run in demo mode — no database, no login service, no AI key configured. Every feature marked "built" has been exercised with sample data. Connecting a real Supabase project and database is the first thing that has to happen, and it will surface bugs.

What the remaining work looks like

In rough order of what unblocks what:

  • Provision real services (Supabase, PostgreSQL, Claude API) and run every feature against them. Fix what breaks.
  • Wire up progress saving so quizzes actually award XP, spend hearts, extend streaks, and feed the dashboard.
  • Curate the videos. 27 topics × 2–4 videos, plus a formula box and short intro per topic. Roughly 80 videos to choose against CAPS. Whoever does this needs to know the syllabus, not the code.
  • Build the question bank. This is now the biggest content job — YouTube does not solve it. 6 questions is a demo, not a product; the target depends on your answer to Q9.
  • Premium and payments, if launching with a paid tier.
  • Deploy: fix the container build, choose a host, set up a staging environment, add basic automated tests before anything is public.

Bottom line

Beta on free tiers: about R99 / year for the domain plus AI usage (tens of rands). Production with a paid tier: ≈ R730 / month fixed (Vercel R325 + Supabase R405) plus AI usage — realistically R1,000–R2,000 / month all-in for a few hundred active students, rising with AI use. The AWS design in the repo, costed as drawn, is ≈ R4,400 / month before traffic (≈ R1,300 trimmed to a bare minimum) — see below — and is not recommended for launch.

Accounts that need to exist, in your name, with your card: Vercel, Supabase, Anthropic, Resend, Cloudflare, a domain registrar, PayFast. We set them up; you own them (Q6).

Questions for you

These are decisions we cannot make on your behalf. Six of them change the size of the job by a large factor — please answer those first; the rest can follow at your pace.

Answer these six first
  1. What is the smallest thing you'd be happy to launch? All of Grades 10–12 in both subjects, one grade, or one subject — and is there a hard date? Grade 12 Maths alone is roughly a quarter of the content work.
  2. Is Angelo English-first, Afrikaans-first, or genuinely bilingual? The interface and AI tutor already work in both. For the videos it matters a lot: the English YouTube ecosystem for CAPS is roughly a hundred times larger than the Afrikaans one, and the best Afrikaans channel blocks in-app playback. "English with Afrikaans where it exists" is one afternoon of extra curation; "every topic in both languages" is a different project. See the Afrikaans section of the video shortlist.
  3. Who curates the videos, and who writes the practice questions? Curation is roughly 80 videos chosen against the CAPS syllabus — we have already found 111 candidates to choose from, so this can be a few hours of review rather than a search. Questions are the larger job and cannot be outsourced to YouTube. Both need a person who knows the syllabus.
  4. Do you need to swap videos and edit topic notes yourself? Today only a developer can change them. A simple editor for video IDs and notes is a modest build; if sending us a list is fine for launch, we skip it.
  5. Are you launching with a paid tier or free first? Nothing exists for payments yet, so "free first" removes an entire workstream from the launch path.
  6. Who owns the accounts we need to test for real — Supabase (login + database), the Claude API key for the tutor, and hosting? Every feature so far has only run on sample data.

Scope — beyond the five above

  1. Do you want a private beta with a handful of students before public launch? If so, from which school(s), and who recruits them?
  2. Is it Mathematics and Physical Sciences only, or should the structure allow for Mathematical Literacy, Life Sciences, or other subjects later? Cheap to plan for now, expensive to bolt on later.
  3. What devices and data conditions are your students on? A video-first curriculum is data-hungry. If most students are on prepaid mobile data, we should keep videos short, default to lower quality, and make the notes and formulas useful without pressing play.

Curriculum content — curation and question decisions

  1. How many practice questions per topic is enough, and is multiple-choice acceptable? Currently 6 questions total. The engine only supports multiple choice; NSC papers are mostly written-answer.
  2. Should questions be adapted from past NSC papers (copyright applies) or written fresh?
  3. Which YouTube channels do you trust for CAPS content? We have prepared a verified shortlist: the ten channels South African learners actually watch, ranked by reach, with 3–4 embeddable candidate videos for every one of the 28 topics. Kevinmathscience (1.1M subscribers, short exam-style clips) is our suggested default. Pick from that list, or tell us which channel to use everywhere.
  4. What makes a video "good enough" for the app — maximum length, South African context, exam-technique focus? We need a rule the curator can apply consistently.
  5. Is the finished Grade 11 Trigonometry topic the right layout — videos, formula box, worked examples, tips? Please review it before we replicate it 27 times.
  6. Who signs off that the videos and formulas are correct and match the syllabus? We need a named subject reviewer.
  7. Should students earn XP for watching a video, and should we track which videos they have watched? Not built yet; easy to add if wanted.
  8. Videos get deleted or made private by their owners without warning. Should we build an automatic check that flags broken videos, or is a periodic manual check acceptable?
  9. The most-watched Afrikaans teacher on YouTube (HouNetOpSukkel, 27.7K subscribers) has switched off in-app playback on almost all their videos. Do you want us to approach them, link out to YouTube for those videos only, or skip them? The app already supports a separate Afrikaans video per slot; 77 embeddable Afrikaans candidates are on the shortlist.
  10. The exam countdown shows fixed 2026 NSC dates. Can you confirm them, and who updates them each year?
  11. The 50 past-paper PDFs are hosted on stanmorephysics.com, a third-party site. Are you comfortable depending on it, or should we host the PDFs ourselves — and are we allowed to?

Premium & payments — only if you answered "paid" above

  1. What is included in Premium, and what does it cost? Today the only difference is unlimited AI hints and photo scans.
  2. Is PayFast confirmed as the provider, is the account live or sandbox, and is it a monthly subscription, once-off, or both?
  3. Free users get 5 AI hints and 1 photo scan per day. Are those the right numbers?

AI tutor — running cost and behaviour

  1. Is the Angelo personality (never gives the full answer, ≤80 words, Socratic, no hollow praise) what you want? Anything to add or remove?
  2. Should students' tutor conversations be stored for you to review, or discarded? Storing them helps you improve content but adds a privacy obligation.

Accounts & learner data — POPIA applies

  1. Students may be under 18. Do you need parental consent at sign-up, and do you have a privacy policy and terms we should link to?
  2. The leaderboard shows other students' first name, last initial and school. Is that acceptable to you and to the schools?
  3. Should sign-in support Google (most learners have a school Google account), or is email + password enough? Password reset is not built yet either way.
  4. Who should be an admin, and do you want any parent or teacher view of a student's progress?

Brand, hosting & ownership — nothing is deployed yet

  1. Is "Angelo" the final name, and do you have a logo? Whose name and logo appear as the issuer on the AI Skills certificate?
  2. Where should the app be hosted, on what domain, and in whose accounts (hosting, DNS, code repository)? Vercel is the fastest path for this stack; an AWS design also exists. Who holds the account and pays matters more than the technology.
  3. Is roughly R730 / month fixed plus AI usage (see Running costs) an acceptable running budget, and who is the billing contact for the seven vendor accounts?
  4. Which email address should welcome, password-reset and certificate emails come from? A Resend key exists but nothing sends yet.

How to choose

  • Two per topic is enough to launch: one short "what is it" clip and one exam-question walkthrough. A third for the hardest topics.
  • Under 20 minutes wins on mobile. A 15-minute 360p video is roughly 40–60 MB; an hour is 200 MB+. Marathons are marked and should not be embedded as-is.
  • Watch the first 60 seconds. Accent, pace and board legibility on a phone matter more than the channel name.
  • Check the grade in the title. Search surfaces Grade 11 videos for Grade 10 queries. Where that happened it is flagged below.
  • Reply with the video codes (the 11-character code at the end of each link) or just say "use the first one everywhere" — either works for us.

Afrikaans lessons

The app can carry a second, Afrikaans video in every slot. We ran the same search in Afrikaans for all 28 topics. The honest summary: there is a real Afrikaans YouTube ecosystem for CAPS Maths and Physics, but it is roughly a hundredth the size of the English one — the biggest channel has 27.7K subscribers against Kevinmathscience's 1.11M, and a typical Afrikaans video has hundreds to low thousands of views rather than hundreds of thousands.

The best Afrikaans channel blocks embedding. HouNetOpSukkel (27.7K subscribers, 1.87M views, 63 videos, Maths and Physics Gr 10–12) is by a wide margin the most-watched Afrikaans teacher — its videos average 20–85K views. But 14 of the 16 HouNetOpSukkel videos we found refuse to play inside other apps; only the two Grade 10 Functions videos allow it. Embedding is a per-video setting the owner controls, so the practical move is to ask them. A small channel with a tutoring business may well say yes to an app that sends them Afrikaans learners.
